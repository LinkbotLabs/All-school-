P4/1 · Week 1

1. Booking a Flight

Real-life situation: Students become a travel agency team. They learn flight-booking vocabulary, build useful questions and sentences, complete a missing-words challenge, then solve different customer itineraries.

Learning objectives: Students can identify key flight-booking vocabulary, ask and answer questions about travel requirements, read an itinerary carefully, compare flight options and explain whether a flight meets a customer's needs.

1. Vocabulary Builder – 7 key words

DepartureThe time a flight leaves.
ArrivalThe time a flight reaches its destination.
DirectA flight with no stops.
StopoverA stop during a journey.
BaggageBags and suitcases.
PassengerA person travelling.
ItineraryA travel plan or schedule.

Quick-fire questions: What do we call the time a plane leaves? What do we call the time it arrives? What is a person travelling called? What is a flight with no stops? What is a travel plan called?

2. Sentence Builder

QuestionPossible answer
Where would you like to travel?I'd like to travel to Japan.
When would you like to depart?I'd like to depart in the morning.
What time would you like to arrive?Before 6 o'clock, please.
Would you like a direct flight?Yes, please. / No, I'd accept a stopover.
Would you accept a stopover?No, I'd prefer a direct flight.
How many passengers are travelling?Three passengers.
How much baggage do you need?We need two bags.
Would you like a one-way or return ticket?A return ticket, please.

Build the sentences: Put the words in the correct order.

  1. like / I / to / Japan / travel / would
  2. direct / prefer / flight / a / I
  3. passengers / are / four / travelling
  4. baggage / need / two / We
  5. arrive / before / I / six / must

3. Missing Words Challenge

Complete each sentence using the word box.

WORD BOX: Departure · Arrival · Direct · Stopover · Baggage · Passenger · Itinerary
  1. The plane leaves at 9 am. This is the __________ time.
  2. The plane reaches London at 5 pm. This is the __________ time.
  3. I don't want to stop anywhere. I want a __________ flight.
  4. Our plane stops in Singapore for two hours. We have a __________.
  5. I have two suitcases. This is my __________.
  6. There are four people travelling. We have four __________.
  7. Our travel schedule is called an __________.

Role-play sentence prompts

Travel Agent: “Good morning. Welcome to World Travel.” “Where would you like to travel?” “When would you like to depart?” “Would you prefer a direct flight?” “How many passengers are travelling?” “How much baggage do you need?” “Would you like a one-way or return ticket?”
Customer: “I'd like to book a flight to…” “I'd prefer to depart in the morning.” “I need to arrive before…” “I'd prefer a direct flight.” “There are … passengers.” “We need … bags.” “I'd like a return ticket, please.”
Booking Manager: “Let me check the available flights.” “This flight departs at…” “It arrives at…” “This flight has one stopover.” “This flight includes … bags.” “Unfortunately, none of these flights meets every requirement.” “I recommend flight…”

Whole-class discussion

Invite each team to report its decision. Ask the whole class: Which team found a direct flight? Which team had a baggage problem? Which customer needed to arrive before 13:00? Which flight had a stopover? Did every customer find a perfect flight? What should a travel agent do when no suitable flight is available?

Quick assessment

Teacher Lesson Plan (50 minutes)

Lesson Objectives: Identify key flight-booking vocabulary; ask and answer questions about departure, arrival, flight type, baggage and passengers; read customer requirements; compare flight options and justify a booking decision.

Vocabulary: Departure, Arrival, Direct, Stopover, Baggage, Passenger, Itinerary.

Summary of Tasks: Vocabulary Builder (7 min) → Sentence Builder (10 min) → Missing Words Challenge (8 min) → Customer Itinerary Challenge (20 min) → Whole-Class Discussion (5 min).

Warm up – Vocabulary Builder

Introduce the seven key words with simple meanings. Use quick-fire oral questions to check understanding. (7 minutes)

Lesson proper – Sentence Builder and Missing Words

Model the booking questions and answers. Students practise putting mixed-up words into correct sentences. Then students complete the seven missing-word sentences using the vocabulary box. Check answers together and correct pronunciation. (18 minutes)

Practice – World Travel Itinerary Challenge

Place students in teams of three: Customer, Travel Agent and Booking Manager. Give each team a different itinerary card and display the shared flight board. Teams read every requirement, identify the best flight or explain why none is suitable, and prepare a short booking conversation. (20 minutes)

Whole-class review

Teams report their decisions. Ask the whole class the review questions and invite students to explain why a flight does or does not meet the customer's requirements. (5 minutes)

Materials/Equipment: Whiteboard, markers and notebooks; teacher display or printout of the flight information board and customer itinerary cards.

References: P4 Practical English Speaking Programme – Topic 1: Booking a Flight; teacher-created fictional flight schedule and customer itinerary challenges.

Take Home Tasks: Write a short 6–8 line conversation between a customer and a travel agent. Include the destination, departure time, arrival time, flight type, number of passengers and baggage.

P4/2 · Week 1

2. Friends & Social Life

Real-life situation: Friends are making plans for the weekend. One friend has a problem and the group must find a plan everyone can enjoy.

Learning objectives: Students can invite someone, make suggestions, agree or disagree politely, explain a problem and reach a group decision.

Seven key vocabulary words

InvitationA request to join an event.
AvailableFree at a certain time.
PlanAn idea about what to do.
SuggestTo give an idea.
PreferTo like one choice more.
CancelTo decide an event will not happen.
ArrangeTo organise something.

Useful expressions

“Are you free this Saturday?”
“Would you like to come with us?”
“How about going to…?”
“I’d rather…”
“That works for me.”
“Sorry, I can’t because…”
“Can we find another time?”
Team challenge: Arrange a weekend activity for a group of friends. You have a total budget of 5,000 baht. Everyone must agree on the plan.

Situation cards

  • Friend A wants to go to the cinema.
  • Friend B wants to play football but has only 500 baht.
  • Friend C has an exam on Monday and needs time to study.
  • Friend D is new to the group and wants to make friends.

Suggested roles (groups of four)

Organiser: Start the conversation, invite everyone and help the group decide.
Friend who loves sport: Suggest a sports activity and explain why you like it.
Busy friend: Explain your study commitment and suggest a suitable time.
New friend: Ask questions, share an idea and help the group include everyone.

Performance task

  1. Perform a natural conversation lasting 3 minutes.
  2. Make at least three suggestions.
  3. Agree on the activity, time, meeting place and budget.
  4. Finish by confirming the plan.

Quick assessment

Teacher Lesson Plan (50 minutes)

Lesson Objectives: Invite a friend, make and respond to suggestions, express preferences politely and agree on a weekend plan.

Vocabulary: Invitation, Available, Plan, Suggest, Prefer, Cancel, Arrange

Summary of Tasks: Warm-up (5 min) → Lesson proper (25 min) → Practice (15 min).

Warm up

Ask: “What do you like doing at the weekend?” Students share one activity with a partner. (5 minutes)

Lesson proper

Teach/review the seven key words. Model inviting a friend and responding positively or negatively. Practise phrases for suggesting activities, explaining availability and changing a plan. (25 minutes)

Practice

In groups, plan a weekend outing. Give each student a different constraint (limited money, busy schedule, different interests or bad weather). Discuss options and agree on a plan everyone can enjoy. Present the plan. (15 minutes)

Materials/Equipment: Whiteboard, markers and notebooks.

References: P4 Practical English Speaking Programme – Topic 2: Friends & Social Life; teacher-prepared weekend-planning situations.

Take Home Tasks: Write five sentences inviting a friend somewhere and explaining the plan.

P4/4 · Week 1

3. Football Fever

Real-life situation: A team has lost an important match 3–2. A reporter interviews the coach and supporters to find out what happened.

Learning objectives: Students can describe a match, give opinions, explain reasons, ask interview questions and respond to different views.

Seven key vocabulary words

MatchA game between two teams.
ScoreThe points or goals in a game.
DefendTo stop the other team scoring.
AttackTo try to score.
RefereeThe person who controls the game.
SupporterA person who cheers for a team.
ImproveTo become better.

Useful expressions

“What did you think of the match?”
“I think the team played well because…”
“In my opinion, the defence was…”
“The referee made a difficult decision.”
“Our team needs to improve…”
“Who was the best player, and why?”
Team challenge: Explain why the team lost 3–2 and present three ideas for improving before the next match. There is no single correct answer: support opinions with reasons.

Match report

  • Final score: Blue Tigers 2 – 3 Red Lions.
  • Blue Tigers scored first.
  • Red Lions scored twice in the second half.
  • A Blue Tigers player received a yellow card.
  • The final goal was scored in the last five minutes.

Suggested roles (groups of four)

Sports reporter: Ask at least four questions and introduce the interview.
Coach: Explain what went wrong and name two things the team will practise.
Supporter: Give an honest opinion about the match and your favourite player.
Player: Describe how the team felt and what you will do differently next time.

Performance task

  1. Present a 3-minute post-match interview.
  2. Include opinions from at least three people.
  3. End with three suggestions for the next match.

Quick assessment

Teacher Lesson Plan (50 minutes)

Lesson Objectives: Talk about football, describe players and match events, give opinions and make a simple team prediction.

Vocabulary: Match, Score, Defend, Attack, Referee, Supporter, Improve

Summary of Tasks: Warm-up (5 min) → Lesson proper (25 min) → Practice (15 min).

Warm up

Ask students to name a football team or player they know. Take a quick class vote: “Who will win the next match?” (5 minutes)

Lesson proper

Present the seven key words and useful expressions. Model describing a match using simple present and past forms where appropriate. Practise asking about teams, positions, scores and opinions. (25 minutes)

Practice

In small groups, students become sports commentators. Give them a fictional match score and events to report. They prepare and deliver a 60–90 second match report, then answer two audience questions. (15 minutes)

Materials/Equipment: Whiteboard, markers and notebooks; optional hand-drawn football pitch.

References: P4 Practical English Speaking Programme – Topic 3: Football Fever; teacher-created fictional match scenario.

Take Home Tasks: Write a short match report (5–7 sentences) about a real or imaginary football match.

P4/6 · Week 1

4. Planning a Holiday

Real-life situation: A group of friends wants to plan a short holiday. They have different interests and a limited budget.

Learning objectives: Students can discuss destinations, compare activities, talk about cost, make suggestions and agree on an itinerary.

Seven key vocabulary words

DestinationThe place you are going to.
AccommodationA place to stay.
ItineraryA plan for a trip.
TransportA way to travel.
ExploreTo visit and learn about a place.
ActivitiesThings to do.
AffordableNot too expensive.

Useful expressions

“Where would you like to go?”
“I’d rather visit…”
“What can we do there?”
“How much will it cost?”
“That’s too expensive for our budget.”
“Let’s make a plan.”
Team challenge: Plan a two-day, one-night trip in Thailand for four friends. Total budget: 8,000 baht for the group. Everyone must have at least one activity they enjoy.

Planning requirements

  • Choose a destination.
  • Decide how to travel there.
  • Choose accommodation.
  • Plan at least three activities.
  • Estimate the total cost and keep within budget.

Suggested roles (groups of four)

Budget manager: Check that the plan stays under 8,000 baht.
Adventure seeker: Suggest exciting activities and explain your choices.
Relaxation fan: Suggest relaxing activities and suitable accommodation.
Trip organiser: Ask questions, keep the discussion moving and present the final itinerary.

Performance task

  1. Present your holiday plan in 2–3 minutes.
  2. Explain the destination, transport, accommodation and activities.
  3. Give an estimated budget and explain why the trip suits the group.

Quick assessment

Teacher Lesson Plan (50 minutes)

Lesson Objectives: Discuss holiday destinations, ask about activities and costs, compare choices and create a simple travel plan.

Vocabulary: Destination, Accommodation, Itinerary, Transport, Explore, Activities, Affordable

Summary of Tasks: Warm-up (5 min) → Lesson proper (25 min) → Practice (15 min).

Warm up

Write “Beach, mountains or city?” on the board. Students choose one and give a reason. (5 minutes)

Lesson proper

Teach/review the seven key words. Model asking about destination, transport, accommodation, activities and budget. Demonstrate comparing two destinations with “cheaper”, “more exciting” and “I would prefer…”. (25 minutes)

Practice

Pairs plan a three-day holiday with a fixed budget. They choose a destination, transport, accommodation and two activities. Each pair presents its itinerary and explains why it suits the budget. (15 minutes)

Materials/Equipment: Whiteboard, markers and notebooks; optional blank itinerary grid.

References: P4 Practical English Speaking Programme – Topic 4: Planning a Holiday; teacher-prepared holiday planning task.

Take Home Tasks: Create a simple three-day holiday itinerary with one sentence for each day.

5. Restaurant Experience

Real-life situation: Friends are eating at a restaurant. They need to order food, ask about the menu and solve a small problem politely.

Learning objectives: Students can order food, ask about ingredients, make requests, explain a problem and respond politely.

Seven key vocabulary words

MenuA list of food and drinks.
OrderTo ask for food or drink.
IngredientsThe foods used to make a dish.
RecommendTo suggest something good.
BillThe money you must pay.
SpicyHaving a hot, strong taste.
AllergyA bad reaction to a food or substance.

Useful expressions

“Could we see the menu, please?”
“What would you recommend?”
“Does this dish contain…?”
“I’d like to order…”
“Could I have this without chilli, please?”
“Excuse me, I think there’s been a mistake.”
“Could we have the bill, please?”
Team challenge: Act out a restaurant visit. The group has 1,000 baht to spend. One customer has a food allergy, and one order arrives incorrectly. Solve both problems politely.

Classroom menu

Food / drinkPrice
Fried rice90 baht
Chicken noodles100 baht
Vegetable salad80 baht
Grilled chicken150 baht
Fruit smoothie60 baht
Water20 baht
Ice cream50 baht

Suggested roles (groups of four)

Waiter: Welcome the customers, explain the menu and take the order.
Customer 1: Ask for a recommendation and order a meal.
Customer 2: Explain that you have a food allergy. Ask clear questions before ordering.
Customer 3: Explain politely that your order is incorrect and ask for help.

Performance task

  1. Perform a 3-minute restaurant conversation.
  2. Include a greeting, menu questions, food orders and the bill.
  3. Resolve the allergy and incorrect-order situations politely.

Quick assessment

Teacher Lesson Plan (50 minutes)

Lesson Objectives: Order food and drinks, ask about menu items, make polite requests and respond to a restaurant problem.

Vocabulary: Menu, Order, Ingredients, Recommend, Bill, Spicy, Allergy

Summary of Tasks: Warm-up (5 min) → Lesson proper (25 min) → Practice (15 min).

Warm up

Ask: “What is your favourite meal?” Students tell a partner and name one ingredient. (5 minutes)

Lesson proper

Introduce the seven key words. Model a restaurant exchange between a customer and server, including ordering, asking for recommendations and requesting the bill. Practise polite forms such as “Could I have…?” (25 minutes)

Practice

Set up groups as restaurant staff and customers. Customers order a meal, ask two questions and respond to a simple problem (wrong order or unavailable item). Swap roles and perform. (15 minutes)

Materials/Equipment: Whiteboard, markers, notebooks and the menu/food examples in the lesson.

References: P4 Practical English Speaking Programme – Topic 5: Restaurant Experience; teacher-created restaurant role-play.

Take Home Tasks: Write a restaurant dialogue of 6–8 lines. Include an order and one polite question.

6. Social Media

Learning objective: Students discuss safe and responsible social media use and create a short positive post.

Seven key vocabulary words

Profile
Follower
Post
Comment
Share
Privacy
Responsible

Useful expressions

“What do you usually post?”
“I think we should protect our privacy.”
“I agree because…”
“I would not share that online.”

Suggested roles

Content creator: introduce a positive post.
Safety adviser: explain two privacy rules.
Audience member: ask questions and give feedback.
Group challenge: Create a 30-second campaign encouraging students to use social media positively. Include one helpful message and two safety tips.

Final speaking task

Give a 60-second presentation: What is your message, who is it for, and how can people stay safe?

Teacher Lesson Plan (50 minutes)

Lesson Objectives: Talk about social media habits, describe benefits and risks, and give advice for safe and respectful online behaviour.

Warm up

Ask: “What can people use social media for?” Students give quick examples without sharing private account details. (5 minutes)

Lesson proper

Teach/review the seven key words. Discuss positive uses and possible problems. Model giving an opinion, agreeing/disagreeing politely and offering advice. Emphasise privacy, kindness and checking before sharing. (25 minutes)

Practice

Groups create a “Think Before You Post” guide with three helpful habits and three safety rules. Each group presents its advice using at least three target expressions. (15 minutes)

Materials/Equipment: Whiteboard, markers and notebooks.

References: P4 Practical English Speaking Programme – Topic 6: Social Media; classroom discussion prompts prepared by the teacher.

Take Home Tasks: Write five online-safety tips using “should” or “shouldn’t”.

Seven key vocabulary words

Reporter
Headline
Event
Witness
Interview
Fact
Opinion

Useful expressions

“This is breaking news.”
“What happened?”
“According to a witness…”
“We have not confirmed that yet.”

Suggested roles

News presenter: introduce the story.
Reporter: describe what happened and where.
Witness: explain what you saw.
Editor: check that the report is clear and sensible.
Group challenge: Create a short news report about a fictional school event, such as a lost-and-found day, sports day or a surprise visitor. Do not present it as real news.

Final speaking task

Deliver a 60–90 second news report with a headline, two facts and one interview.

Teacher Lesson Plan (50 minutes)

Warm up

Write “What makes news interesting?” on the board. Students suggest examples of school or community news. (5 minutes)

Lesson proper

Introduce the seven key words. Explain the basic structure of a news report: what happened, where, when, who was involved and what is known. Model cautious language and the importance of checking facts. (25 minutes)

Practice

Groups receive or invent a harmless fictional school-news event. They prepare a short report covering the key details and present it as news reporters. The audience asks two questions. (15 minutes)

Materials/Equipment: Whiteboard, markers and notebooks; teacher-created fictional news prompt.

References: P4 Practical English Speaking Programme – Topic 9: Breaking News; teacher-prepared fictional news scenario.

Practice

Groups choose a scenario: delayed flight, missing luggage or wrong boarding gate. Passenger, airport staff and travel companion work through the problem and perform a 2–3 minute conversation. (15 minutes)

Materials/Equipment: Whiteboard, markers and notebooks; optional hand-drawn boarding pass or luggage tag.
