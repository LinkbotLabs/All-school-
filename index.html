P4 • Practical English Speaking Programme

Week 1: Real-Life English Challenges

Five different lessons for five classes. Students work in teams, make decisions, ask follow-up questions and communicate in realistic situations.

50 minutes per lessonStudent-led learningPair & group speakingSeven key words per lesson

How to run every 50-minute lesson

0–5 min · Situation

Introduce the real-life scenario. Ask students what they already know.

5–10 min · Language

Teach or elicit the seven key words and useful expressions.

10–15 min · Challenge

Explain the task, form groups and assign roles.

15–30 min · Prepare

Students plan, research if permitted, practise and solve the challenge.

30–45 min · Perform

Groups perform. Audience members listen and ask one question.

45–50 min · Improve

Give brief feedback. Students repeat one improved line or explain their decision.

Teacher role: set up the task, monitor, support when needed and give focused feedback. Aim for students to do most of the talking. Phone use is optional and should support the task rather than replace speaking.

P4/1 · Week 1

1. Booking a Flight

Real-life situation: A customer needs to book a flight. A travel agent helps compare options, while a friend gives advice.

Learning objectives: Students can ask for travel information, compare choices, explain preferences and agree on a suitable flight.

Seven key vocabulary words

DepartureThe time a flight leaves.
ArrivalThe time a flight reaches its destination.
DirectA flight with no stops.
StopoverA break between flights.
BaggageBags taken on a trip.
BudgetThe money available to spend.
AvailableFree to book or use.

Useful expressions

“I’m looking for a flight to London.”
“How much does it cost?”
“Does the price include checked baggage?”
“Is there a direct flight?”
“I’d prefer the cheaper option because…”
“That sounds like the best choice.”
Team challenge: Find or invent two possible Bangkok–London flight options. Choose the one that best matches the customer's needs. No booking or payment is required. If phones or internet are unavailable, use the sample options below.

Customer requirements

  • Budget: no more than 25,000 baht.
  • Departure: Friday.
  • Maximum one stopover.
  • Total journey: no more than 18 hours.
  • Must include checked baggage.
  • Must arrive before 6:00 p.m.

Suggested roles (groups of three)

Customer: Explain your needs. Ask at least four questions. Decide which flight suits you.
Travel agent: Offer two options. Explain price, departure, arrival, stopover and baggage. Ask what matters most to the customer.
Friend: Give advice. Ask about the customer's priorities and explain which option you recommend.

Sample flight options (use if no live research)

OptionPriceDepartureArrivalStopsBaggage
A24,500 bahtFriday 09:00Friday 23:301Included
B21,000 bahtFriday 06:00Friday 22:001Not included

These are fictional classroom examples, not real flight offers. Students should notice that both arrive after 6 p.m.; they can ask the agent to find another option.

Performance task

  1. Perform a 2–3 minute booking conversation.
  2. The customer must ask questions and make a final choice.
  3. The group must explain why the chosen flight is best.

P4/2 · Week 1

2. Friends & Social Life

Real-life situation: Friends are making plans for the weekend. One friend has a problem and the group must find a plan everyone can enjoy.

Learning objectives: Students can invite someone, make suggestions, agree or disagree politely, explain a problem and reach a group decision.

Seven key vocabulary words

InvitationA request to join an event.
AvailableFree at a certain time.
PlanAn idea about what to do.
SuggestTo give an idea.
PreferTo like one choice more.
CancelTo decide an event will not happen.
ArrangeTo organise something.

Useful expressions

“Are you free this Saturday?”
“Would you like to come with us?”
“How about going to…?”
“I’d rather…”
“That works for me.”
“Sorry, I can’t because…”
“Can we find another time?”
Team challenge: Arrange a weekend activity for a group of friends. You have a total budget of 5,000 baht. Everyone must agree on the plan.

Situation cards

  • Friend A wants to go to the cinema.
  • Friend B wants to play football but has only 500 baht.
  • Friend C has an exam on Monday and needs time to study.
  • Friend D is new to the group and wants to make friends.

Suggested roles (groups of four)

Organiser: Start the conversation, invite everyone and help the group decide.
Friend who loves sport: Suggest a sports activity and explain why you like it.
Busy friend: Explain your study commitment and suggest a suitable time.
New friend: Ask questions, share an idea and help the group include everyone.

Performance task

  1. Perform a natural conversation lasting 3 minutes.
  2. Make at least three suggestions.
  3. Agree on the activity, time, meeting place and budget.
  4. Finish by confirming the plan.

P4/4 · Week 1

3. Football Fever

Real-life situation: A team has lost an important match 3–2. A reporter interviews the coach and supporters to find out what happened.

Learning objectives: Students can describe a match, give opinions, explain reasons, ask interview questions and respond to different views.

Seven key vocabulary words

MatchA game between two teams.
ScoreThe points or goals in a game.
DefendTo stop the other team scoring.
AttackTo try to score.
RefereeThe person who controls the game.
SupporterA person who cheers for a team.
ImproveTo become better.

Useful expressions

“What did you think of the match?”
“I think the team played well because…”
“In my opinion, the defence was…”
“The referee made a difficult decision.”
“Our team needs to improve…”
“Who was the best player, and why?”
Team challenge: Explain why the team lost 3–2 and present three ideas for improving before the next match. There is no single correct answer: support opinions with reasons.

Match report

  • Final score: Blue Tigers 2 – 3 Red Lions.
  • Blue Tigers scored first.
  • Red Lions scored twice in the second half.
  • A Blue Tigers player received a yellow card.
  • The final goal was scored in the last five minutes.

Suggested roles (groups of four)

Sports reporter: Ask at least four questions and introduce the interview.
Coach: Explain what went wrong and name two things the team will practise.
Supporter: Give an honest opinion about the match and your favourite player.
Player: Describe how the team felt and what you will do differently next time.

Performance task

  1. Present a 3-minute post-match interview.
  2. Include opinions from at least three people.
  3. End with three suggestions for the next match.

P4/6 · Week 1

4. Planning a Holiday

Real-life situation: A group of friends wants to plan a short holiday. They have different interests and a limited budget.

Learning objectives: Students can discuss destinations, compare activities, talk about cost, make suggestions and agree on an itinerary.

Seven key vocabulary words

DestinationThe place you are going to.
AccommodationA place to stay.
ItineraryA plan for a trip.
TransportA way to travel.
ExploreTo visit and learn about a place.
ActivitiesThings to do.
AffordableNot too expensive.

Useful expressions

“Where would you like to go?”
“I’d rather visit…”
“What can we do there?”
“How much will it cost?”
“That’s too expensive for our budget.”
“Let’s make a plan.”
Team challenge: Plan a two-day, one-night trip in Thailand for four friends. Total budget: 8,000 baht for the group. Everyone must have at least one activity they enjoy.

Planning requirements

  • Choose a destination.
  • Decide how to travel there.
  • Choose accommodation.
  • Plan at least three activities.
  • Estimate the total cost and keep within budget.

Suggested roles (groups of four)

Budget manager: Check that the plan stays under 8,000 baht.
Adventure seeker: Suggest exciting activities and explain your choices.
Relaxation fan: Suggest relaxing activities and suitable accommodation.
Trip organiser: Ask questions, keep the discussion moving and present the final itinerary.

Performance task

  1. Present your holiday plan in 2–3 minutes.
  2. Explain the destination, transport, accommodation and activities.
  3. Give an estimated budget and explain why the trip suits the group.

P4/8 · Week 1

5. Restaurant Experience

Real-life situation: Friends are eating at a restaurant. They need to order food, ask about the menu and solve a small problem politely.

Learning objectives: Students can order food, ask about ingredients, make requests, explain a problem and respond politely.

Seven key vocabulary words

MenuA list of food and drinks.
OrderTo ask for food or drink.
IngredientsThe foods used to make a dish.
RecommendTo suggest something good.
BillThe money you must pay.
SpicyHaving a hot, strong taste.
AllergyA bad reaction to a food or substance.

Useful expressions

“Could we see the menu, please?”
“What would you recommend?”
“Does this dish contain…?”
“I’d like to order…”
“Could I have this without chilli, please?”
“Excuse me, I think there’s been a mistake.”
“Could we have the bill, please?”
Team challenge: Act out a restaurant visit. The group has 1,000 baht to spend. One customer has a food allergy, and one order arrives incorrectly. Solve both problems politely.

Classroom menu

Food / drinkPrice
Fried rice90 baht
Chicken noodles100 baht
Vegetable salad80 baht
Grilled chicken150 baht
Fruit smoothie60 baht
Water20 baht
Ice cream50 baht

Suggested roles (groups of four)

Waiter: Welcome the customers, explain the menu and take the order.
Customer 1: Ask for a recommendation and order a meal.
Customer 2: Explain that you have a food allergy. Ask clear questions before ordering.
Customer 3: Explain politely that your order is incorrect and ask for help.

Performance task

  1. Perform a 3-minute restaurant conversation.
  2. Include a greeting, menu questions, food orders and the bill.
  3. Resolve the allergy and incorrect-order situations politely.
